# Review research, round 2: what #80's five pain points leave out

2026-10-06. Read-only research for #80. Round 1 (§1 of #80) had five complaints: throughput dies in review, approval is theatre, the review answers how and not why, most comments are noise, and the tooling loses the thread. This round looks for what those five miss, on the author's side, the reviewer's and the team's, and checks each against the board that shipped in 0.17.0 (DESIGN.md §37: findings and questions, lanes by state, a person answers questions and rules on contested findings, Post asks each owner to post its own items).

How to read it:
- Evidence is a number or a paraphrase. There is one direct quote in the whole file, to keep within the quoting limit; everything else in the Evidence columns is my wording.
- "New" means #80 §1 doesn't name it. "Partly" means #80 touches it but not this side of it. "Already" rows are kept only where the new source adds a number.
- Anything I couldn't confirm from the primary source is marked **unverified**.
- A lot of the best evidence is from 2026 arXiv preprints, not yet peer-reviewed. They are marked "preprint".

## 3. The biggest gaps between the pain and the board

1. **The board shows the argument, not the change.** A reviewer starts by orienting: what is this for, how big is it, where is the risk. The board has none of that: no intent, no risk map, no reading order. So it can list what the agents disagree about but can't say "here is the 10% to look at", which is the maintainer's point 7 from today. Since June 2026 CodeRabbit (Overview, Review), Meta (ARCTIC's intent, drift and Spotlight) and Microsoft (PR summaries) all lead with intent and ranked regions.

2. **Silence isn't shown, and silence is where the bugs are.** The board holds only what was raised. Nothing records what the agents checked and found fine, or which risky files nobody looked at. The controlled study (2411.11401) found that people review where the AI points and stop there: more small findings, no more serious ones. A person who reads only the cards will miss what the agents missed. Claims with evidence (#80 §2) were the answer to this and didn't ship.

3. **Evidence is prose, and "fixed" is the owner's word.** Reviewers ask for proof it runs: a test plan, commands and output, screenshots or video, tests that fail without the change, CI not weakened. The board takes Markdown text and a commit hash. The raiser never confirms a fix, and a new push doesn't mark anything out of date. A settled board can describe code that's no longer on the PR.

4. **Agents agreeing isn't agents being right.** LLMs give way to a rebuttal that comes as the next turn, a convincing PR description talks AI reviewers out of real vulnerabilities, and same-model reviewers share blind spots. On the board, the author's side can contest and the raiser can then drop, or concede, and a person never sees it. Only an open dispute reaches "Needs you". There is no rule for an independent reviewer, no path-based gate (auth, CI config, migrations always need a person), and no record of who verified what.

5. **"Needs you" is addressed to nobody in particular, and the result ends up scattered.** Asking a team instead of one named person slows review (Meta, −11.6% when one person is named). The board doesn't name an accountable person, doesn't ping anyone when a question is asked, and Post produces one review per owner, not one record a person signed off. Nothing on it checks that a human now understands the change, which is the knowledge-transfer job reviewers say AI reviews don't do.
